# Practice Tab — Design Description

> Saved from the provided paste. Practice is where the student uses what they
> learned. Cases, questions and role-plays are made from the student's
> materials and shaped by their weak spots. Three small tabs: **Cases** ·
> **Questions** · **Communication**. Questions made in Materials also show
> here; this tab adds a wider mix across materials and weak spots.

---

## Cases

**Purpose.** The student works through a realistic case step by step. They make
decisions and explain their thinking, and the app gives feedback.

### Start screen (top to bottom)

- **Header:** Title "Cases."
- **Two big buttons:** Clinical and Public health.
- **Make a case from my material:** A row of tap cards for the student's
  materials. For example, a lecture on waterborne diseases becomes a cholera
  outbreak case.
- **Suggested for you:** Cases on weak topics, marked "Needs review," and on
  topics for an upcoming exam.
- **Case type (tap buttons, no dropdowns):**
  - Clinical: History-taking, Symptom analysis, Differential diagnosis,
    Choosing investigations, Interpreting findings, Clinical decision-making,
    Management plan
  - Public health: Outbreak investigation, Environmental hazard,
    Health promotion plan, Community intervention, Epidemiological
    investigation, Population-level decision
- **Difficulty:** Easy, Medium, Hard. Starts on Medium, then adapts.
- **Voice:** A switch to run the case by voice.
- **A bold "Start case" button.**
- **Unfinished case:** A "Continue" card at the top.
- **First time:** "Pick a case type or add a material, and I'll make a case
  from it."

### Case screen

- **Top bar:** Back arrow, the case title, a step counter like "Step 3 of 7,"
  a voice button, and a close button.

**How a case goes.**

1. The case starts with only a little information.
2. The student makes a decision. They pick an option or type an answer.
3. The student explains their reasoning in a short box before moving on.
4. The app gives feedback.
5. More information appears, like a real case unfolding.
6. This repeats until the case ends.

Information appears in floating cards, one at a time, with a soft shadow and
no outlines. Earlier cards stay above, so the student can look back.

**Clinical case steps:** patient arrives with main complaint → history-taking
(student picks which questions to ask) → symptom analysis → examination
findings → choosing investigations → interpreting findings (results, scans,
tables) → differential diagnosis (possible causes, likeliest first) → clinical
decision and management plan.

**Public-health case steps:** alert/report comes in → confirming the problem
and setting a case definition → looking at the data (case list, graph over
time, map) → forming ideas about the cause → choosing investigation steps →
choosing control measures or an intervention → communicating with the community
→ judging the result.

**Tools on the screen.** Notes box (the student can jot down thoughts). Hint (a
small link; using one is noted). Ask the tutor (a small link for a stuck
concept). Voice: a "Run this case by voice" button; the case is read aloud one
step at a time; the student answers and explains by speaking; captions and a
transcript show on screen; switching between voice and typing any time.

**Feedback after each decision.** What was good about the choice; what was
missed; why the better choice is better; a small tag if it shows a knowledge
gap, with an "Ask the tutor about this" link; the page or slide it relates to,
when the case came from the student's material.

**Adapting.** Cases get harder or easier based on decisions. Weak spots from
Progress shape which cases come up. Topics for an upcoming exam come first.
Cases use the student's course and level from their profile.

**Labels.** From your material (parts built from the student's own lecture or
notes). AI-made scenario (the case itself is a made-up teaching scenario).
Outside source (with a link, when guidelines or evidence are used). When the
app isn't sure, it says so.

**End of a case.** A summary card shows what went well, what was missed,
knowledge gaps found, and next steps like "Review cholera control measures,
then try a harder outbreak case, review again in 3 days." Bold buttons: "Learn
this with the tutor" and "Try another case."

**Where results go.** Progress (gaps and strengths update). AI Tutor (Teach Me
explains the gaps). Questions (practice questions on the weak topics). Study
Plan and Home (revision and review times added).

**Example:** upload lecture on waterborne diseases → cholera outbreak case →
gaps found → Teach Me → revision scheduled.

**Messages.** "No material yet: Add a material to make a case from it, or pick
a case type." · "Case failed to build: We couldn't make this case. Try another
type." · "Voice failed: Voice isn't working right now. You can keep typing." ·
"Saving failed: We couldn't save your progress. Try again."

**Not on this screen:** No dropdowns, streaks, or badges.

## Questions

**Purpose.** Test what the student knows, across all their materials. This tab
is the wider version of the Practice Questions tab in Materials. Materials
tests one material at a time. This tab mixes materials and is shaped by the
student's weak spots.

### Start screen (top to bottom)

- **Header:** Title "Questions."
- **Suggested for you:** Cards for questions on weak topics (marked "Needs
  review"), topics due for review, and topics for an upcoming exam. Each card
  has a bold "Start" button.
- **Quick set:** A bold "5 questions" button for a short session.
- **Build your own set (all tap buttons, no dropdowns):** Material (row of tap
  cards, or "All"); Topics (tap buttons; weak marked "Needs review"); Type
  (Practice questions, Application questions, Diagram labeling); Difficulty
  (Easy, Medium, Hard — starts Medium, then adapts); How many (5, 10, 20, or
  30); Mode (Relaxed with hints and per-answer explanations, or Timed
  exam-style with answers at the end). A bold "Start practice" button.
- **Past sets:** List with name, date, score; Redo or Review mistakes on each.
- **Empty screen:** "Add a material and I'll make questions from it." Button to
  Materials.

**Question types.** Practice questions (MCQ and short answer, one idea at a
time). Application questions (short scenario, then what-to-do/what-it-means —
these connect to Cases). Diagram labeling (a diagram from the material with
labels to fill in — tap a label and pick or type the answer).

**Question screen.** Counter like 7/20, close button, timer in Timed mode.
Question in the middle, choices below. "Show hint" link (Relaxed only). Bold
"Check answer" button.

**After each answer (Relaxed).** Right/wrong right away. Plain explanation of
why, and why the other choices are wrong. The page or slide it came from. "Ask
the tutor" link. Bold "Next" button. **Timed mode:** no feedback until the end;
the student can flag a question to come back to.

**Results screen.** Score. Topics that went well, to review, and needing
attention. Every question with its explanation. Wrong answers become
flashcards. Bold buttons: "Review wrong answers" and "Practice again." A next
step like "Review regression basics, do 5 more questions, review again in 3
days."

**Adapting.** Harder/easier with answers. Weak topics from Progress and AI
Tutor get more questions. Upcoming-exam topics first. About-to-forget topics
return on spaced intervals. Course and level from profile.

**Labels.** From your material (with page/slide). AI example. Outside source
(with link). When unsure, say so.

**Where results go.** Progress, AI Tutor (Teach Me), Flashcards (wrong answers
become cards), Study Plan and Home (review times).

**Messages.** "No material yet…" · "There isn't enough material for 30
questions. Try 10." · "We couldn't make questions from this. Try again." ·
"We couldn't save your results. Try again."

**Not on this screen:** No dropdowns, streaks, or badges.

## Communication

**Purpose.** Role-play space for clinical and public-health communication.
Voice is a major part — speaking is closer to real practice than typing.

### Start screen (top to bottom)

- **Header:** Title "Communication."
- **Two big buttons:** Clinical and Public health.
- **Who you'll talk to (tap cards):** Patient, Caregiver, Community member,
  Colleague, Community leader (public health).
- **Scenario (tap buttons, no dropdowns):** Clinical — History-taking, Patient
  interview, Health education, Motivational interviewing, Breaking bad news.
  Public health — Public-health communication, Community engagement, Explaining
  an outbreak, Explaining a health measure.
- **Personality (tap buttons):** Anxious, Angry or frustrated, Confused, Quiet
  or reluctant, Talkative, Skeptical of healthcare, Worried about cost. Plus a
  "Surprise me" button.
- **Challenge (tap buttons, pick any):** Language or culture barrier,
  Misunderstanding, Sensitive questions, Patient refuses an exam, Difficult
  questions, Community distrusts an intervention.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, and Exam (OSCE).
- **Mode:** Text or Voice. Voice is shown first.
- **From my material:** A row of tap cards. A scenario can be made from the
  student's lecture or notes.
- **Suggested for you:** Scenarios on weak Progress skills, marked "Needs
  practice."
- **A bold "Start" button.**
- **Documentation:** A small link, "Practice a SOAP note."
- **Past sessions:** List with scenario, date, score. Redo and See feedback.
- **First time:** "Pick who you'll talk to and a scenario, and we'll start."

### Before the conversation

A short brief card shows the setting and what the student knows (e.g. "You are
in a clinic. A 45-year-old has had chest pain for two days."). In Exam mode the
brief is shorter and a timer appears.

### Conversation screen

**Top bar:** Back arrow, character name and role, scenario, voice button, and
(in Exam mode) a timer.

**Text mode.** Floating bubbles, like the AI Tutor chat. The character answers
realistically based on what the student says. Good questions open the
character up; cold or rushed approaches make them pull back or get upset.

**Voice mode.** The student speaks, the character answers out loud. Captions
and a transcript show on screen. A big round microphone button sits at the
bottom. Switching between voice and text any time.

**How the character reacts.** Anxious (needs calm, clear words). Angry (needs
to feel heard first). Confused (needs plain words, no jargon). Quiet (needs
patient, open questions). Talkative (needs steering back on track). Skeptical
(needs trust built step by step). Worried about cost (needs cost raised
kindly). Challenges: language/culture barrier, misunderstanding, sensitive
question, refused exam, hard question, distrustful community.

**Tools (not in Exam mode).** Hint, Pause, Ask the tutor.

**Ending.** The student taps "End session," or it ends at the scenario goal.
In Exam mode, when time runs out.

### Feedback

After the session, a summary card with four parts: what went well (good
questions, kind words, clear explanations); what was missed (information never
asked about or said); what could be said differently (examples with a better
way to say it); what to practice next (one or two skills). Also: scores for
Clarity, Questioning, Empathy and professionalism, and Overall; question
order (right questions in the right order, not only polite words); transcript
with per-part comments.

**Exam (OSCE) mode.** Timer, short brief, no hints. Scored against a visible
criteria checklist: done and not-done shown at the end. Feedback only at the
end.

**Documentation practice (SOAP notes).** After a history-taking session, write
a SOAP note (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan) from what happened. The
tutor checks for missing parts and unclear writing, and shows what a good note
looks like for this case. Also practicable alone, from a short case summary.

### Adapting

Difficulty follows performance. Weak Progress skills shape scenarios. Course
and level from profile.

### Labels

From your material (parts built from the student's lecture or notes). AI-made
scenario (character and story made up for teaching). Outside source (with a
link, when guidelines are used).

### Where results go

Progress (communication skills update alongside other topics). AI Tutor (Teach
Me can explain a weak skill). Study Plan and Home (practice times added).

### Messages

"Nothing yet: Pick who you'll talk to and a scenario to begin." · "Voice
failed…" · "Microphone blocked: Allow the microphone to speak. You can use
text instead." · "Saving failed: We couldn't save your session. Try again."

**Not on this screen:** No dropdowns, streaks, or badges.

---

## Summary

- **Cases:** step-by-step clinical and public-health simulations with feedback
  and voice support.
- **Questions:** mixed practice across materials, Relaxed and Timed modes.
- **Communication:** role-play with adaptive personalities, voice, OSCE mode.
- All three use weak spots from Progress, adapt difficulty live, include voice
  options, and feed Progress, AI Tutor, Study Plan and Home.
