# Materials Tab — Design Description
## "Study it. Practice it. Anchor it."

> Saved from the provided paste. Materials is where everything starts: the
> student adds their own material, the app reads it and builds study tools,
> and those tools feed AI Tutor, Practice, Study Plan and Progress.

**Flow:** Upload → Analyze Docs → Notes, Summaries, Flashcards, Practice
Questions (cases are made too, and show in Practice).

**Small tabs:** Upload · Analyze Docs · Notes · Summaries · Flashcards ·
Practice Questions.

---

## 1. Upload

**Purpose.** Add the student's own material. When added, it goes to Analyze Docs.

**Layout, top to bottom.**

- **Header:** Title "Upload" and a small search box to find a material by name.
- **Add area:** A large box: "Drop files here or tap to choose." A line under
  it: "PDF, slides, textbook pages, audio, images, and notes." A bold "Add
  material" button. Below it, small buttons: Take a photo, Record audio, Paste
  text.
- **Connect:** Buttons for Google Drive, OneDrive, Moodle, Canvas, Blackboard,
  Notion. Tapping one asks the student to allow access, then lets them pick
  files. The student decides what the app can see and can disconnect any time.
- **Your materials:** A list, newest first. Each row has the file icon, name,
  subject, date, and a status (Uploading, Ready to analyze, Analyzing, Done, or
  Needs attention). A small menu: Rename, Change subject, Download, Delete.
- **Empty screen:** "Add your first lecture." One line: "We'll turn it into
  notes, flashcards, a summary, and practice questions." The same bold button.

**Adding a file.**

- Pick one or more files.
- Each file shows a progress bar while it uploads.
- The name fills in by itself, and the student can change it.
- Pick a subject from a row of tap buttons, or add one.
- A bold "Analyze" button sends the files to Analyze Docs. "Save for later"
  keeps them without analyzing.

**Rules.** Several files at once. A file can go to an existing or new subject.
An upload can be stopped any time.

**Privacy.** A small line near the add area: "Your files are private and only
you can see them." Delete removes the file and everything made from it, after
the student confirms. A "What we collect and why" link sits at the bottom.

**Messages.**

- Too big: "This file is too large. Try splitting it into smaller parts."
- Not supported: "We can't read this kind of file yet."
- Upload stopped: "Upload stopped. Tap to try again."
- Connection failed: "We couldn't connect to Drive. Try again."

## 2. Analyze Docs

**Purpose.** The app reads a material and shows what is in it. The student then
picks what to make.

**Layout, top to bottom.**

- **Header:** Title "Analyze Docs" and a row of tap cards for the uploaded
  materials, with the chosen one marked.
- **Not analyzed yet:** The name, subject, file type, and a bold "Analyze"
  button. If nothing is uploaded: "Upload a material first," with a button to
  Upload.
- **While it reads:** A progress list, ticked off as each step finishes:
  Reading the file; Finding topics and subtopics; Finding key ideas,
  definitions, and formulas; Finding tables and diagrams; Finding how ideas
  connect. The student can leave the screen, and a bell reminder says when it
  is ready.
- **What we found:** Counts at the top (topics, concepts, definitions,
  formulas, tables, diagrams). Then sections the student can open: Topics and
  subtopics (with a checkmark on each; the student can rename or remove a
  topic), Key concepts, Definitions, Formulas (each in its own box), Tables,
  Diagrams, Important facts, How ideas connect (a simple map), Hard areas (each
  with a small tag), Course words (so the app uses the same words as the
  course).
- **Choose what to make:** Rows of tap buttons, each starting on a sensible
  choice: Detail (Brief, Standard, Deep), Style (Simple, Technical, With
  examples), Difficulty (Easy, Medium, Hard), Goal (Understand, Exam prep, Quick
  review), Make (Notes, Summaries, Flashcards, Practice questions, Cases — all
  on by default). A bold "Generate" button builds everything from the checked
  topics. A small "Analyze again" link redoes the reading.

**After Generate.** Notes, Summaries, Flashcards, and Questions fill their
tabs. Cases go to Practice. The Tutor can teach from the material. Review times
go to Study Plan. Results go to Progress.

**Messages.**

- Hard to read: "Some pages are hard to read. Try a clearer photo."
- Nothing found: "We couldn't find topics in this file. Try another file."
- Failed: "We couldn't finish reading this. Try again."

## 3. Notes

**Purpose.** Clear revision notes made from the materials. The student can also
write their own.

**Layout, top to bottom.**

- **Header:** Title "Notes," a small search box, and a bold "Add my own note"
  button. Two small buttons: Made for you and My notes.
- **Notes list:** Grouped by material. Each row has the title, subject, and
  date. A star marks favorites. A small tag shows the type: Topic breakdown,
  Revision notes, or Formula walkthrough.
- **Empty screen:** "Notes show up here after you analyze a material." A button
  goes to Analyze Docs.

**Opening a note.**

- Top: Title, subject, and the material it came from. A "Detail" row of tap
  buttons (Brief, Standard, Deep). A "Listen" button.
- Topic breakdown: Short parts with clear headings, from the big idea to the
  details.
- Concept explanations: Plain words first, then the technical term.
- Diagrams: Shown inside the note, with labels. Tap to enlarge.
- Formulas: Each in a highlighted box, with what each letter means and when to
  use it.
- Formula walkthrough: A worked example. The student taps "Next step" to reveal
  each step.
- Key terms: In bold. Tapping one shows its meaning.
- Quick review: A short box at the end with the main points.
- Actions: Select any line and tap "Ask the tutor." Bold buttons at the bottom:
  "Make flashcards from this" and "Practice this."

**Writing my own note.** A simple writing area with headings, bold, lists, and
pictures. The student chooses the material or subject. App notes can be edited
too. The original stays, and the student's version is marked "Edited."

**Messages.**

- Nothing yet: "No notes yet. Analyze a material to make some."
- Failed: "We couldn't make notes from this. Try again."

## 4. Summaries

**Purpose.** Short, high-yield summaries for fast review.

**Layout, top to bottom.**

- **Header:** Title "Summaries" and a small search box.
- **Summaries list:** Grouped by material. Each row has the title, subject,
  date, and reading time. A small tag shows the type: High-yield summary or
  Quick review. A star marks favorites.
- **Empty screen:** "Summaries show up here after you analyze a material." A
  button goes to Analyze Docs.

**Opening a summary.**

- Top: Title, subject, and the material it came from. Two small buttons:
  High-yield and Quick review. A "Listen" button with a speed control (slow,
  normal, fast). Captions and a transcript show while it plays.
- High-yield summary (one page): Main points (short lines, most important
  first), Key terms (in bold, with a plain meaning), Formulas (each in its own
  box, with when to use it), Diagram (one, if the material has one), Watch out
  (common mistakes or hard parts, with a small tag).
- Quick review (last-minute study): A short list of facts, one line each,
  about 2 minutes to read. Tapping a line opens the full part of the note.
- Actions: Select any line and tap "Ask the tutor." Bold buttons: "Make
  flashcards from this" and "Practice this."

**Messages.**

- Nothing yet: "No summaries yet. Analyze a material to make one."
- Failed: "We couldn't make a summary from this. Try again."
- Audio failed: "Audio isn't working right now. You can still read the summary."

## 5. Flashcards

**Purpose.** Help the student remember. Cards come back at the right time.

**Layout, top to bottom.**

- **Header:** Title "Flashcards," a small search box, and a bold "Add a card"
  button.
- **Today box:** "Due today: 14 cards" and a bold "Start review" button.
- **Decks list:** One deck per material. Each row has the name, subject, and
  card count, plus small counts for New, Due today, Learned. A tag shows weak
  topics: "Needs review."
- **Empty screen:** "Flashcards show up here after you analyze a material." A
  button goes to Analyze Docs.

**Inside a deck.** Title, subject, counts, a bold "Start review" button, and a
list of cards with a small search. Each card can be edited, moved, or deleted.

**Review screen.** A counter at the top, like 12/20, and a small close button.
One big card showing a question or term, with "Tap to flip" under it. After
the flip, the answer shows, with a small "Ask the tutor" link. Two bold
buttons: Wrong and Correct. A "Listen" button reads the card aloud.

**Card types.** Term and meaning; Question and answer; Formula (the formula,
what each letter means, and a worked example); Concept (a short explanation to
recall in the student's own words).

**When cards come back.** Wrong: sooner, in the same session or the next day.
Correct: after 2 days, then a week, then later. Cards on weak topics come back
more often. Cards for an upcoming exam come first. The student can add a note
on any card.

**End of review.** The score, topics that went well, topics to review, and
"Next review: tomorrow." Times go to Study Plan, and results go to Progress.
Bold buttons: "Review wrong cards" and "Practice this."

**Making your own cards.** A simple form with front, back, and the material or
subject. Cards can also be made from any note or summary line.

**Messages.**

- Nothing yet: "No flashcards yet. Analyze a material to make some."
- Nothing due: "You're done for today. Next review is tomorrow."
- Failed: "We couldn't make flashcards from this. Try again."

## 6. Practice Questions

**Purpose.** Test what the student learned.

**Layout, top to bottom.**

- **Header:** Title "Practice Questions" and a small search box.
- **Start a practice set:** Material (a row of tap cards, or "All"), Topics
  (tap buttons for the topics found; weak topics marked "Needs review"), Type
  (Practice questions, Application questions, Diagram labeling), Difficulty
  (Easy, Medium, Hard), How many (10, 20, or 30). A bold "Start practice"
  button.
- **Past sets:** A list with the name, date, and score. A small button on each:
  Redo or Review mistakes.
- **Empty screen:** "Questions show up here after you analyze a material." A
  button goes to Analyze Docs.

**Question types.** Practice questions (multiple choice and short answer, one
idea at a time); Application questions (a short scenario, then a question about
what to do or what it means — these connect to cases in Practice); Diagram
labeling (a diagram or image from the material with labels to fill in — the
student taps a label and picks or types the answer).

**Question screen.** A counter at the top, like 7/20, and a small close button.
The question in the middle, with answer choices below. A small "Show hint"
link. A bold "Check answer" button.

**After each answer.** Right or wrong right away. A plain explanation of why,
and why the other choices are wrong. The page or slide it came from. A small
"Ask the tutor" link. A bold "Next" button.

**Results screen.** The score, topics that went well, and topics to review.
Wrong answers become flashcards. Bold buttons: "Review wrong answers" and
"Practice again." Review times go to Study Plan, and results go to Progress.

**Messages.**

- Nothing yet: "No questions yet. Analyze a material to make some."
- Failed: "We couldn't make questions from this. Try again."
- Not enough material: "This material is too short for 30 questions. Try 10."

## Shared across Materials

**Labels on every note, card, summary, and question.**

- From your material: with the page or slide, so the student can check it.
- AI example: an example the app made up.
- Outside source: with a link.
- When the app isn't sure, it says so.

**Personalization (from the PRD).** Uses the student's level, course, past
results, weak spots, and upcoming exams. Follows the detail, style, difficulty,
and goal chosen in Analyze Docs. Weak topics from Progress get a small "Needs
review" tag and extra practice. Items made for an exam say so: "Made for your
Biostatistics midterm."

**Review reminders.** Notes, summaries, and flashcards show "Review again in 2
days," then a week. Times go to Study Plan and Home.

**Connections.**

- AI Tutor: teaches from the materials. "Ask the tutor" is on every tab.
- Practice: uses the cases and questions.
- Progress: records how the student does.
- Study Plan: gets the review times.

**Style.** Light background, soft shadows, almost no outlines. The teal gradient
only on bold buttons. Book-style headings and plain text for everything else.

**Not on any Materials screen.** No dropdowns, streaks, or badges.
